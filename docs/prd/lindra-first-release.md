# Lindra first release: admission assessment and visit drafts from the physician's notes or dictation

Terms are as defined in `CONTEXT.md`. Decisions referenced as ADR NNNN live in `docs/adr/`. Research behind them lives in `docs/research/`.

## Problem Statement

Ella is the physician in an inpatient multimodal pain-therapy team. After she has assessed a newly admitted patient she has to turn everything she learned, the patient's reports, prior findings, her own findings, the pain problems, the current and previous treatments, the functional and psychosocial impact and her reasoning for inpatient treatment, into the physician-authored sections of the admission documentation in the hospital system, in the order of the ward's assessment form. Then, on every visit during the treatment period, she writes a short progress note that has to stay consistent with what she documented before. Today she does this by hand against a template: she sorts her notes, writes every section out, and checks nothing was lost. It is the single biggest block of organising work in her day, it competes with time for patients, and the sorting is clerical while the content is hers.

The tools on the market record consultations ambiently and write generic notes. She does not want her patient conversations recorded, her hospital system stays the record, and nothing exists for the structure of a pain-therapy admission assessment and its visits.

## Solution

Lindra is a German web application on her iPad and desktop. From the sidebar she picks a case under a fictional case identifier, or creates one with its treatment period, and opens an encounter: the admission assessment or a dated visit. She puts her notes in by typing or pasting free text, or by dictating her own notes after the assessment and correcting the transcript. When she asks for it, Lindra proposes a draft of that encounter's sections, written only from what she supplied and, for a visit, from what she had already accepted for the assessment and earlier visits. She reviews one section per step, with the current draft and the proposal side by side; every paragraph shows which passage of the notes, or which earlier encounter, it came from. Whatever the notes did not say, or said twice in contradiction, lands on a clarification list instead of in the draft. She edits the draft, accepts or discards the proposal as a whole, and in the last step copies each section as plain text into the hospital system.

Lindra never invents diagnoses, interpretations, causes or justifications, never ranks pain problems, never recommends treatment and never assesses doses (ADR 0001). It runs on self-hosted models with no cloud path in the product (ADR 0003); a hosted text model may be compared against the self-hosted one in the fictional-case evaluation only. The first release is evaluated only on three fictional cases (ADR 0006); its target is at least 30 percent less total preparation time, including review and correction, with no fabricated clinical statement and no lost relevant fact.

## User Stories

Cases, encounters and notes

1. As the physician, I want to create a case with a case number, a required patient name, an optional short label, the date and time of the admission assessment and the inclusive treatment period, so that I recognise the case later without Lindra holding any further demographics. (Evaluation release: number and name are fictional.)
2. As the physician, I want the sidebar to list my cases by case number with the patient name as subtitle, starting with "Fall auswählen" and no case preselected, so that I find the right patient quickly and never work in the wrong case by accident.
3. As the physician, I want to reopen a case and find every encounter's notes, draft, proposal state and clarification list exactly as I left them, so that interrupted work is not lost.
4. As the physician, I want to delete a case, so that nothing lingers once the documentation is in the hospital system.
4a. As the physician, I want a selected case to show its admission assessment and its dated visits with "Neue Visite", and a selected encounter to show its review steps beneath, so that I can jump from patient to patient and from section to section.
4b. As the physician, I want to create a visit whose date is constrained to the treatment period, defaulting to now when now lies inside it and to nothing otherwise, with the time free within the day and backdating allowed, so that a visit never lands outside the stay and I never accept a wrong default.
4c. As the physician, I want to correct the treatment period later, and to be refused with the conflicting encounters named when a shorter period would strand a visit or the assessment, so that nothing is deleted or moved silently.
5. As the physician, I want to type or paste free-form notes for each encounter in any order without a mandatory question-by-question form, so that I can work the way the assessment or the visit went.
6. As the physician, I want my notes autosaved while I type, so that I never lose them to a closed tab or a dead battery.
7. As the physician, I want a clear indication in the status area when my changes are not saved because the network is gone, with a separate retry button, so that I know what to retype rather than assuming it was kept.
7a. As the physician, I want connection, saving, generation-in-progress and failure shown in one top-right status area that never moves my buttons and never looks like a button itself, so that feedback never costs me a mis-tap.
7b. As the physician, I want the buttons of a step to keep their size and place while the content scrolls, with the microphone aligned to them, so that my hands learn where things are.
8. As the physician, I want each encounter to keep its own notes and its own draft, never appended to the assessment and never rewriting an earlier encounter, so that the record of what I documented when stays true.
9. As the physician, I want the application in German on both my iPad and my desktop browser, so that it fits where I actually write.
9a. As the physician, I want a calm light screen with readable text, large touch targets, visible keyboard focus and states that do not rely on colour alone, so that I can work on it for hours and on an iPad with my finger.
9b. As the physician, I want the sidebar beside the work on a wide screen and as a drawer behind a button on a portrait iPad, so that switching case or encounter is always one tap away.
9c. As the physician, I want states shown as small icons with the explanation on tap, and only the consequential ones, "Veraltet" and "Nicht aktualisiert", spelled out on the active section, so that the screen stays calm and the text is mine, not Lindra's.
9d. As the physician, I want a disabled Übernehmen to show its reason next to it, so that I never wonder why I cannot accept.

Dictation

10. As the physician, I want to explicitly start and stop a dictation of my own notes from any step of any encounter through a persistent control, so that nothing is recorded that I did not mean to record and I never have to leave the review to dictate.
11. As the physician, I want recording, transcribing and transcript review to run in a full overlay that blocks everything else until I accept or discard the transcript, so that I always know a microphone is open and never leave one running behind other work.
12. As the physician, I want the transcript shown for review and correction before it joins my notes, so that recognition errors never reach a draft.
13. As the physician, I want an accepted transcript appended to the selected encounter's notes rather than replacing text I typed, and a pending proposal to become outdated by it rather than silently regenerated, so that dictating and typing can be mixed without surprises.
14. As the physician, I want the audio discarded once the transcript exists, so that no audio archive of my dictations accumulates.
14a. As the physician, I want a denied microphone permission or a failed transcription told to me in the overlay with a way out, so that a device problem never leaves me stuck or silently loses my words.
15. As the physician, I want dictation to work from my iPad's microphone in the browser, so that I can dictate where I assessed the patient.
16. As the physician, I want speech recognition to run self-hosted, so that my dictation never leaves our own hardware.

Generation and the draft

17. As the physician, I want a draft generated only when I explicitly ask for it, so that nothing changes under my hands.
18. As the physician, I want the admission assessment draft organised in the clinical headings and order of our assessment form, Sozialanamnese, Frühere Anamnese und Vormedikation, Therapeutische Anamnese, Schmerzanamnese, Internistische, vegetative, psychiatrische und Suchtmittelanamnese, Untersuchungsbefunde, Behandlungsplan und vereinbarte Ziele, so that it maps onto what I document in the hospital system.
18a. As the physician, I want a visit draft with only four short headings, Verlauf seit letzter Visite, Aktuelle Befunde, Ärztliche Einschätzung, Vereinbartes Vorgehen und Ziele, with empty headings left empty and listed for clarification, so that a progress note stays short and never gets padded.
18b. As the physician, I want a visit draft to draw on the accepted assessment and the accepted earlier visits as background, never on later visits or pending proposals, with such paragraphs marked as coming from an earlier encounter, so that a backdated visit reads as of its date and today's observations stay distinguishable from history.
18c. As the physician, I want a visit draft or proposal flagged "Hintergrund seit Erstellung geändert" when I accepted a change to the assessment or an earlier visit after it was generated, with the text kept, the proposal refused, and regeneration only on my action, so that history is never rewritten behind my back.
18d. As the physician, I want to review one section per step with the current draft and the proposal side by side, reachable in any order from the sidebar, so that I can read the comparison comfortably and still jump where I need to.
19. As the physician, I want a new draft offered as one proposal for the whole encounter that I accept or discard with one action, so that reviewing a generation costs me one decision, not one per section.
20. As the physician, I want to edit the draft freely, so that the final wording is mine.
20a. As the physician, I want sections I edited by hand to survive accepting a proposal, including edits I made while the proposal was pending, with the skipped sections named before I accept and labelled "manuell bearbeitet, nicht aktualisiert" afterwards, so that my wording is never overwritten and I never believe the whole draft is fresh when it is not.
20b. As the physician, I want the proposal's alternative for a skipped section to stay visible until I finally accept or discard the proposal, so that I can take it over by hand if I want it.
20c. As the physician, I want accepting a proposal to adopt its clarification list for the encounter, and my manual edits never to tick off a clarification item by themselves, so that open questions stay open until I resolve them.
21. As the physician, I want one marker on the whole encounter saying its draft is possibly outdated when I change the notes after generating it, without Lindra guessing which sections are affected, so that I know when to regenerate and am not misled by guesses.
21a. As the physician, I want a proposal that was generated from older notes, because I typed, accepted a transcript or changed notes while it was being generated, to stay visible but be unacceptable and marked "Notizen seit diesem Vorschlag geändert", so that I discard it and regenerate deliberately instead of accepting text that no longer matches my notes.
21b. As the physician, I want badges on section headings and sidebar entries that mean only "proposed change", "manually edited", "not updated", "open clarification" or "copied", so that I see what needs my attention without any badge pretending to verify content.
22. As the physician, I want my notes and my last draft preserved when a generation fails, so that a model error costs me nothing.
23. As the physician, I want each generated paragraph to carry a tappable input reference that reveals the passage of my notes, or the earlier encounter, it came from, without a confidence score or any claim that it is correct, and to lose that reference when I rewrite the paragraph, so that origin is visible and never mistaken for verification.
24. As the physician, I want a final handover step, and a small copy control on each section step, that copies a single section of the current draft as plain text with one action and a visible acknowledgement, never the pending proposal, so that pasting into the hospital system is fast and keeps the hospital's formatting.
24a. As the physician, I want an encounter without an accepted draft to say plainly that there is nothing to copy yet, so that I never paste an empty section.
24b. As the physician, I want to see that a generation is in progress and to keep reading while it runs, so that a slow model never looks like a dead button.
25. As the physician, I want the text model to run self-hosted with no cloud fallback, so that patient information never leaves our own hardware.
25a. As the operator, I want to compare the self-hosted text model against a hosted one on the fictional cast only, in a configuration I launch explicitly, so that I learn whether local inference is good enough without any real data or default cloud path.

Clinical fidelity

26. As the physician, I want every pain problem kept separate with its site, radiation, onset and history, time course, intensity, quality, physical and psychological modifiers, coping, functional limitations and goals, without a fixed number of pains, so that the draft does not blur two pains into one and does not force a third.
27. As the physician, I want the main pain to be the one I named and none otherwise, so that Lindra never ranks problems for me.
27a. As the physician, I want treatment decisions and goals in a visit worded only as I supplied them, so that Lindra never recommends or infers a clinical decision.
28. As the physician, I want the patient's reports, prior findings and my own findings kept apart in the draft, with dates and time windows preserved when I gave them, so that provenance in the letter stays true.
29. As the physician, I want a statement whose provenance I did not give to stay without a source rather than get an invented one, so that the draft never claims more than I said.
30. As the physician, I want current and previous treatments kept separate, each with the substance, dose, duration, effect, adverse effects and reason for stopping that I supplied, so that the therapy history is complete and mine.
31. As the physician, I want no dose inferred, converted or interpreted and no interaction or recommendation added, so that the draft never contains a clinical judgement I did not make.
32. As the physician, I want the inpatient justification worded only from what I supplied, and left on the clarification list when I supplied nothing, so that the letter never argues for admission in my name.
33. As the physician, I want missing or contradictory information collected on a clarification list while every supported section is still drafted, so that one gap does not block the rest.
34. As the physician, I want no guessed normal findings, no fabricated diagnoses and no silently resolved contradictions, so that I can trust that everything in the draft came from me.
35. As the physician, I want no claim of medical completeness or physician approval anywhere in the draft, so that the responsibility stays visibly mine.
36. As the physician, I want scores I entered, such as a questionnaire result, reproduced with instrument, version and date when I gave them, so that the letter is precise without Lindra scoring anything itself.

Evaluation and operation

37. As the physician, I want three fictional German cases ready in Lindra, each with a treatment period, an admission assessment and visits, so that I can judge it without touching a real patient.
38. As the physician, I want to compare my preparation time with Lindra, for the assessment and for visits, including review and correction and including transcript correction when dictating, against my template workflow, so that the release is judged on time saved and facts kept.
39. As the operator, I want a throwaway stack per ticket, a long-lived test environment on `origin/main` and an operator-run production, so that nothing a ticket does can touch production and nothing in test is real.
40. As the operator, I want the test environment and every automated test to run on deterministic stand-ins for the text model and speech recognition, so that tests prove the workflow without a GPU and without a real model.
41. As the operator, I want a separately and explicitly launched real-backend configuration, self-hosted or the hosted comparison, that only ever sees the fictional cast, so that model quality can be evaluated without that becoming the default.
42. As the operator, I want the application reachable only on the private network with a single protected login, so that no registration or role system is needed for one author.

## Implementation Decisions

Shape

- One Python web service with a SQLite database and a server-driven, responsive German browser UI for iPad and desktop (ADR 0005). No native app, no offline synchronisation.
- Access is the private network (Tailscale) plus one protected application login; a single token or password, no registration, no roles.
- The hospital system is the record; Lindra hands over plain-text sections by copy and has no integration, export or sending (ADR 0002).
- The screen (ADR 0008, prototype `case-flow.html` plus the grill 5 corrections): a sidebar with cases (case number, patient name as subtitle), then the selected case's encounters with their status badges, then the selected encounter's review steps; in portrait a drawer. The main area shows "Fall auswählen" with no case selected, the case overview (number, name, label, treatment period with "Ändern", encounters, "Neue Visite") with a case selected, and otherwise the current step: notes, one section per step with the current draft and the proposal in two large panes (stacked in portrait), and a final step that is "Übernehmen" while a proposal is pending or "Übergabe" otherwise. Case creation, period correction and new visit are modal forms. Dictation is a persistent control, aligned with the other controls, that opens a blocking overlay.
- Status and controls (ADR 0008, grill 5): a top-right status area carries connection, saving, generation-in-progress and failure indicators without shifting the layout and without button styling; recovery actions such as retry are distinct buttons. There is no bottom status flyout. Each step uses the available height; content scrolls under a stable action bar whose buttons keep their size and position across steps, in landscape and portrait. States are icons with an explanation on tap; "Veraltet" and "Nicht aktualisiert" are spelled out on the active section; a disabled acceptance shows its reason visibly.
- States every implementation ticket must cover although the prototype did not show them: generation in progress, microphone permission denied, failed transcription, failed save with retry, and an encounter without an accepted draft to copy.
- UI foundation (ADR 0009): a single light theme, Quiet Clarity (warm off-white, opaque writing surfaces, forest-teal actions, restrained local SVG icons), on daisyUI 5 component CSS over native semantic HTML controls, pinned and self-hosted with fonts and icons; no React or Web Awesome runtime, no CDN assets, no glass effects, no dark theme, no rich-text editor, no onboarding. daisyUI is conditional on a target-browser check; if it fails, the findings go back to the operator with a Bootstrap alternative. Accessibility baseline: visible keyboard focus, non-colour state cues, reduced-motion behaviour, touch targets of at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, contrast validated in the real token combinations.
- Browser support: the latest two major Safari and iPadOS releases, current desktop Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari. Older iPads are not promised.
- Brand and writing (ADR 0009, grill 6): the operator's brand kit "Editorial Brackets" supplies the palette (Teal, Sage, Ink, Paper, White) and the logo; tokens derive from it after contrast checks, the wordmark does not dictate the UI font. Every UI ticket reads the unslop skill and its signs before work and checks its final diff against them; proof names the signs checked and remaining findings with reasons. The same discipline applies to changed app copy, documentation and reports.

Domain model

- A case holds a case number (primary identifier), a required patient name (subtitle; fictional in the evaluation release), an optional label, the inclusive treatment period, exactly one admission assessment and any number of visits (ADR 0010). No further demographics and no patient-management workflow. Cases list, create, open, correct the period and delete. A period correction is refused when an encounter would fall outside; the refusal names the encounters. Encounters are never deleted or moved by a period change.
- An encounter (assessment or visit) holds its date and time (inside the period), its notes with a notes revision, its current draft with the notes revision and, for a visit, the background revision it was generated from, a per-section manually-edited flag, the pending proposal (if any) with its notes and background revisions, the clarification list, an acceptance counter and per-section copied flags. A new visit's date defaults to now when now lies in the period and is otherwise empty; backdating within the period is allowed.
- The background of a visit is the accepted drafts of the assessment and of visits dated before it; its background revision is the sum of their acceptance counters. Later visits and pending proposals are never background.
- Notes are one editable text body per encounter. Every saved change advances the notes revision. A transcript is appended to the selected encounter's notes as a marked passage once the physician accepts it; it never replaces existing text.
- A draft has exactly the fixed sections of its encounter kind: for the assessment the seven form-aligned sections (ADR 0010), for a visit the four visit sections. Each section holds paragraphs; each generated paragraph carries an input reference (a span of the encounter's notes, or a span of a background encounter's accepted draft marked as historical); a paragraph the physician rewrote carries none. Within the Schmerzanamnese, pain problems are separate with no fixed count; the main pain only when named.
- The clarification list is a list of items per encounter, each naming the section it affects and the missing or contradictory information, in the physician's language. A contradiction between background and today's notes is a clarification item, never resolved silently.
- Structured content the draft must respect: pain problems (site, radiation, course, intensity, modifiers, limitations, main pain only when named), provenance (patient report, prior finding, physician finding, unknown), dates and time windows when supplied, treatments (current or previous; substance, dose, duration, effect, adverse effects, discontinuation reason, each only when supplied), user-entered scores with instrument, version and date when known.

Generation contract

- Generation runs only on the physician's explicit action, per encounter. The service sends the encounter's notes, and for a visit its background, to the text model adapter and receives a structured result: sections with paragraphs and input references (notes span or background encounter span), and the clarification list. Prose with no structure is rejected as a failed generation.
- A generation result is stored as one proposal for the whole draft of that encounter next to the current draft, bound to the notes revision and, for a visit, the background revision it was generated from. The physician accepts or discards it with one action; there is no per-section acceptance (ADR 0008).
- Acceptance replaces only sections without the manually-edited flag, including sections edited while the proposal was pending. The API reports the sections it would replace and skip before acceptance and the final step names them; after acceptance the skipped sections keep their flag and the badge "manuell bearbeitet, nicht aktualisiert". Acceptance adopts the proposal's clarification list for the encounter, increments the encounter's acceptance counter (which changes the background revision of later visits) and clears the copied flags; manual edits never resolve clarification items.
- When the current notes revision differs from the proposal's, or for a visit the current background revision differs from the proposal's, the proposal is outdated: it stays visible, the API refuses to accept it, and the UI marks it with the reason ("Notizen seit diesem Vorschlag geändert" or "Hintergrund seit diesem Vorschlag geändert"). The physician discards it and regenerates explicitly; Lindra never regenerates on its own, and an outdated proposal never clears a stale state.
- When the notes revision moves past the one the current draft was generated from, the draft is stale: one encounter-wide marker says it is possibly outdated. When a visit's background revision moves past the one its draft was generated from, the draft carries "Hintergrund seit Erstellung geändert"; the text is preserved. No per-section staleness is guessed. A stale state clears only when a proposal generated from the current revisions is accepted; sections skipped on acceptance stay marked as not updated.
- Section badges carry exactly five meanings: the pending proposal changes this section, the section is manually edited, it was not updated on the last acceptance, the clarification list has an open item for it, it was copied. Encounter badges in the sidebar carry: proposal pending, proposal outdated, draft possibly outdated, background changed. None expresses verification; every badge reveals its explanation on tap.
- Handover copies sections of the current accepted draft only, never the pending proposal.
- A failed generation leaves notes and current draft untouched and tells the physician it failed and why, in plain words.
- The prompt and post-processing enforce the intended purpose (ADR 0001): no inference of diagnoses, causes, justification, doses, interactions, normal findings or provenance; no ranking of pain problems; no resolution of contradictions; no completeness or approval claims. The structured result is checked for input references that do not point into the notes and for sections the notes did not support; such paragraphs are dropped and the gap goes on the clarification list.

Adapters

- A text model adapter with three implementations: the fixture model (deterministic, keyed to the fictional cast, used by all tests and by the test tier), a self-hosted model adapter speaking to a local inference server on the same host or network, and a hosted-inference adapter (Ollama Cloud was named) that is usable only in the explicitly launched evaluation configuration and refuses to start anywhere else (ADR 0003). The product configuration has no cloud option and no fallback. Whether the named provider runs inference in Europe is unverified and, even if true, does not make it local.
- A speech-to-text adapter with two implementations: canned transcripts keyed to fixture audio, and a self-hosted recogniser (candidates from research: whisper.cpp, faster-whisper) chosen after evaluation on the available hardware with German medical text. Audio is held in memory or a temporary file only for the duration of transcription.
- The browser captures audio with explicit start and stop from a persistent control on every step, runs recording, transcription wait and transcript review in a full overlay that blocks all other interaction, uploads the audio for transcription and appends the accepted transcript to the selected encounter's notes. Browser microphone capture requires a secure origin (HTTPS), which the private-network setup must provide.
- Which concrete models fit the hardware and reach acceptable German quality is unverified and is settled inside an implementation ticket with a recorded result, not assumed by this spec.

Seams for tests

- Primary seam: the application's HTTP API with the fixture model and canned transcripts. Every workflow test goes through it: case creation with period validation, period correction refused with named conflicts, visit creation inside and outside the period and backdated, per-encounter notes autosave and revisions, dictation and transcript review appended to the right encounter, generation per encounter with background limited to earlier accepted encounters, proposal acceptance with skipped manually-edited sections, refusal of a proposal outdated by notes or by background, stale and background-changed markers, failure preservation, input references (notes and historical) and their loss on rewrite, clarification list adoption, handover copy text of the current draft only.
- Pure logic tests where the behaviour is a function: the result check (dropping unsupported paragraphs and unreferenced spans), the acceptance merge (which sections are replaced, which retained and labelled), outdated-proposal detection from notes and background revisions, background selection for a visit by date, period conflict detection, default visit date, staleness, transcript append, section plain-text rendering.
- One browser flow on an iPad-sized portrait viewport and a desktop viewport: start with no case selected, create a case with number, patient name and period, open the assessment, type notes, dictate from a review step with the overlay blocking navigation, generate, hand-edit one section, accept with that section retained and badged, change notes while a proposal is pending and see acceptance disabled, create a backdated visit inside the period and be refused outside it, generate the visit and see a historical reference, accept a change to the assessment and see the visit's background badge, switch encounters through the portrait drawer, copy a section from the handover step and see the acknowledgement, open an encounter without an accepted draft and see that nothing can be copied, simulate a failed save and retry from the status area. The flow also checks keyboard focus visibility, touch-target size, and that the action bar's buttons keep their position and size across steps and while status text changes.

Environments (ADR 0007)

- Throwaway stack per ticket in rootless Docker on its node; long-lived test on Linux 790 as user and node `lindra-test`, port 8780, compose project `lindra-test`, running only `origin/main`, refreshed every 5 minutes by a user timer; production run by the operator as `frogger`.
- One compose file and one `scripts/env.py` with `up`, `check`, `reset`, `down`, `ensure-test`, `install-timer`. `reset` seeds the fictional cast only. Nodes read `LINDRA_TEST_URL` and `LINDRA_TEST_TOKEN`.
- The test tier always runs the stand-ins. A real-backend evaluation configuration, self-hosted or the hosted comparison, is launched separately and explicitly; the hosted provider's account and key stay with the operator, who approved the comparison but no spending.
- The production host for real clinical use is an open decision pending the legal and organisational check; installing production does not authorise real data (ADR 0006).

## Testing Decisions

- A good test drives the HTTP API or the browser like the physician would and asserts only what she would observe: what is saved, what the draft contains, what the clarification list says, what the copied text is, what the dictation state shows. Tests do not assert on prompts, database rows or adapter internals.
- Every automated test uses the fixture model and canned transcripts. These prove the workflow and the fidelity rules Lindra enforces itself (dropping unsupported paragraphs, staleness, no overwrite). They do not prove medical fidelity of a real model or real recognition quality; nobody may cite them as such.
- The fictional cast is the test data everywhere: the three cases from ADR 0006, each with a treatment period, an admission assessment and at least one visit (one backdated), notes, expected fixture drafts, expected clarification items and, for dictation, fixture audio with a canned transcript. One case carries an assessment change accepted after a visit draft, so the background-changed state is in the data. The third case expects gaps, so a test that passes by filling them is wrong.
- Modules under test: cases, periods and encounters, notes, dictation and transcript review, generation and proposals including background, the result check, handover copy text, the environment script's `check` and `reset`.
- Prior art: none in this repository; the scaffold ticket establishes the test runner and the first API test, which every later ticket follows.

Operator-run checks (no factory node can run them; each names when)

- **Operator-run, before merge of the scaffold ticket:** on Ella's physical iPad in Safari over the private network, open the scaffold's sample page built on the chosen CSS distribution and confirm it renders, controls are tappable at 44 by 44 CSS pixels, keyboard focus is visible with an external keyboard, and reduced motion is respected. This is the compatibility check ADR 0009 requires before the CSS distribution is committed to; a failure goes back to the operator with a Bootstrap alternative.
- **Operator-run, before merge of the dictation ticket:** on Ella's physical iPad in the browser over the private network, start a dictation from a review step, see the overlay block the rest of the app, stop, see the transcript, correct it and accept it into that encounter's notes. Confirms microphone capture, secure origin and the overlay on the real device.
- **Operator-run, before merge of the real-backend ticket:** on the chosen inference hardware, run the self-hosted model and recogniser against the fictional cast, and the hosted comparison with the operator's own account, and record the measured German quality and latency in the repository; a result that cannot carry the workflow is reported as such, not hidden.
- **Operator-run, at the end of the release:** Ella prepares the three fictional cases, assessment and visits, with her template workflow and with Lindra, typed and dictated, measuring total time including review, correction and transcript correction, and judges fabricated statements, lost facts, consistency between visits and the assessment, and usability. The result is recorded in the repository; a missed target creates follow-ups and is not weakened.
- **Operator-run, at install of test:** the operator checks Linux 790 for suitability (no production service, rootless Docker, port 8780 free) before the test environment goes there.
- **Operator-run, at install of production:** the operator deploys to the host they name following the runbook and confirms `env.py check` passes; this confirms the evaluation configuration only.

## Out of Scope

- Team accounts, roles, collaborative editing; other disciplines as authors.
- Schedules and planning. (Visits with their own progress drafts are in scope since grill 4; the earlier exclusion of ongoing progress documentation is withdrawn.)
- Final discharge synthesis or epicrisis; treatment-plan generation; any clinical recommendation or inferred clinical decision.
- Digitising the assessment form's checkboxes; a fixed number of pain problems.
- A research assistant; a full electronic record.
- Hospital system integration of any kind: FHIR, eArztbrief, KIM, HL7, GDT; PDF or Word export; sending letters; taking over hospital-issued case numbers.
- Patient management: demographics beyond the name, search across patients, merging or transferring cases.
- PDF upload or OCR of forms, a patient portal, complete questionnaire capture, automatic scoring or psychological interpretation.
- Per-section acceptance of a proposal; automatic regeneration; guessing which sections are stale.
- A dark theme, glass or refraction effects, a rich-text editor, onboarding, generated artwork, logo or brand work; a React or Web Awesome runtime; runtime CDN assets; older iPads outside the support policy.
- Ambient recording or any recording of patient conversations; operating-system or browser dictation.
- Cloud text models in the product, including as a fallback; cloud speech recognition in any form. The only hosted inference is the fictional-case evaluation comparison (ADR 0003).
- Native Apple app; offline synchronisation.
- Use with real patient data; this is an evaluation release (ADR 0006).

## Further Notes
